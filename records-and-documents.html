Records & Documents

Every strong claim starts with paperwork. Here’s exactly which records matter, where they live, and how to get your hands on them — free of charge.

You don’t need to pay anyone to retrieve your military or VA records. The steps below link directly to the official government sources that hold them.

The six documents that matter most

Together, these tell the complete story of your service and your health — the story the VA uses to decide your claim.

Proof of Service

DD-214 (Report of Separation)

The single most important military document you own. It proves your service, discharge character, dates, and awards — and it unlocks nearly every VA benefit.

How to get it ↓
Personnel File

OMPF (Official Military Personnel File)

Your full personnel record: enlistment papers, orders, evaluations, awards, and training. It fills in the gaps when your DD-214 alone isn’t enough.

How to get it ↓
In-Service Medical

Service Treatment Records (STRs)

Sick-call visits, injuries, and diagnoses while you served. For most disability claims, this is the evidence that connects a condition to your service.

How to get it ↓
Your VA File

C-File (Claims File)

Everything the VA has on you: past claims, rating decisions, C&P exam results, and evidence. Essential before filing an appeal or an increased-rating claim.

How to get it ↓
Ongoing Care

VA Medical Records

Lab results, notes, and treatment history from VA care. Download them yourself, any time, from the My HealtheVet experience on VA.gov.

How to get it ↓
Supporting Evidence

Private Records & Buddy Statements

Civilian doctor records, plus written statements from family, friends, or the people you served with. These fill the gaps official files leave behind.

How to request each record

Five steps, in the order most veterans need them. Start with the DD-214 — nearly everything else references it. On a phone or tablet, tap a step to expand it.

  • Fastest: use the National Archives’ eVetRecs online tool and choose “Make a new request.”
  • By mail or fax: complete Standard Form 180 (SF-180) and send it to the address listed for your branch and dates of service.
  • Recently separated? You can also request personnel documents through milConnect (DPRIS) once your file is complete.
  • It’s free. The Archives warns against companies that charge a fee for this service.

Found an error? Fix the record.

Wrong dates, missing awards, an unjust discharge characterization — there is a form and a board for each. All are free to file.

DD Form 215
Clerical Corrections

For simple administrative fixes to your DD-214 — a misspelled name, a wrong date, an omitted award. Requested through your branch’s personnel office, and typically the fastest route.

Correction guidance at Archives.gov →
DD Form 149
Board for Correction of Military Records

For substantive errors or injustices — including discharge upgrades after 15 years. File with your branch’s correction board (BCMR/BCNR) with supporting evidence. Boards generally expect filing within 3 years of discovering the error, but can waive it in the interest of justice.

Download DD Form 149 (PDF) →
DD Form 293
Discharge Review (Within 15 Years)

If you separated within the last 15 years and believe your discharge characterization was improper or inequitable, your branch’s Discharge Review Board is usually the first stop — and often faster than a BCMR.

Get organized — and stay that way

Records arrive slowly and claims move fast. Set up your system once, and every future form gets easier.

Protect the originalsStore your DD-214 and original documents somewhere fireproof and safe. Never mail your only copy anywhere.
Digitize everythingScan each document to PDF. Name files clearly: DD214.pdf, STRs-2004-2008.pdf, Rating-Decision-2019.pdf.
Keep two backupsOne cloud copy and one USB drive — so a lost laptop never means a lost record.
Build a service timelineOne page listing units, dates, deployments, and when symptoms or injuries began. It becomes the spine of every claim.
Log every requestWrite down what you requested, when, and any confirmation number. The Archives asks you to wait 90 days before checking status.
Read your DD-214 closelyCheck block by block — name, dates, awards, discharge character. Catching an error now is far easier than during a claim.
