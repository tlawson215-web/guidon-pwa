The three review lanes at a glance

Disagree with a VA decision? Since 2019 there are exactly three ways to challenge it — each built for a different situation.

Supplemental Claim
You have new evidence · Form 20-0995

The VA reopens your case and reviews it together with new and relevant evidence it hasn’t seen before — new records, a medical opinion, a buddy statement.

File any time — within 1 year of the decision to protect back pay. VA’s goal: about 125 days.
Official page at VA.gov →
Higher-Level Review
The VA made an error · Form 20-0996

A more senior reviewer takes a fresh look at the same record to find mistakes. No new evidence allowed — but you can request a phone call to point out the error.

Must file within 1 year of the decision. VA’s goal: about 125 days.
Official page at VA.gov →
Board Appeal
Judge review · Form 10182

A Veterans Law Judge in Washington, D.C. reviews your case — the most thorough option, and the slowest. Three dockets: Direct Review, Evidence Submission, or Hearing.

Must file within 1 year of the decision. Expect a year or more, depending on docket.
Official page at VA.gov →
1 year.

The deadline that protects your money. File any review within one year of the date on your decision letter and you keep your effective date — meaning if you win later, your back pay reaches all the way back to your original claim, even if the fight takes years. Miss the year and the decision becomes final: you can still file a Supplemental Claim, but the clock — and the back pay — starts over.

You don’t have to decide alone

Before you pick a lane — especially for an appeal — a trained second set of eyes is worth more than any website.

Free, accredited help

Accredited Veterans Service Organizations (VSOs), claims agents, and attorneys are trained and recognized by the VA to help with claims and appeals. VSO help is always free — and they can often see what’s actually in your file.

Find accredited help at VA.gov →

What paid help can — and can’t — charge

Accredited agents and attorneys generally can’t charge you anything for an initial claim. For appeals, their fee comes out of back pay they win for you — if you don’t win, you don’t pay. Never pay upfront fees to “claim consultants.”

Denied after a Higher-Level Review? File a Supplemental Claim with new evidence. Denied again? Take it to the Board. As long as each filing lands within one year of the last decision, your effective date — and your back pay — stays alive the whole way.
The old decision is final, but you are not out of options: a Supplemental Claim can be filed at any time with new and relevant evidence — just know the effective date restarts from the new filing. The one narrow exception is a Clear and Unmistakable Error (CUE) in the original decision, which is rare and hard to prove — ask an accredited VSO whether it applies before chasing it.
The VA’s stated goal is about 125 days for both Supplemental Claims and Higher-Level Reviews. Board Appeals are measured in months to years — Direct Review is the fastest Board docket, a hearing the slowest. New C&P exams, informal conferences, and evidence rounds all add time. Whatever lane you choose, you can track the status on VA.gov.
